                                    Raghuvanshi 2025 - 2026 Happy Diwali106Retirement Reversed: More Pensioners Returning to Work to Combat LonelinessBy Councillor Ameet Jogia MBE We can see firsthand from the fantastic work of the Lohana Social Club, that the era of traditional retirement, with peaceful days spent relaxing, after decades of hard work are long behind us. For many pensioners, retirement has left a distorted image of routine, purpose, and companionship. Resulting in a higher return to work, not merely due to the financial benefits but to combat the feeling of loneliness.With our communities constantly changing there is an influx of the older generation returning to work. This choice is one that reflects the values of purpose and hard work of our community. Especially, in an age of exponentially growing technology, it is often assumed that the younger generation possess all the answers. However, older members of our community are the silent pillars for resilience, loyalty, and patience. Their return to work is a true testament to the British Indian values we all hold.Their reintegration into employment should be viewed through a positive lens, as an opportunity. One that not only advances physical health but contributes to overall improved mental well-being. In fact, Rex Fan, Bupa%u2019s lead behavioural insights specialist said work strengthens and builds the neurological pathways in our brains, which can lower the risks of diseases such as Alzheimer%u2019s. Beyond these individual benefits, the contributions elderly people make whether this is through paid employment or voluntary work simultaneously to improve our wider society. Whilst we must recognise the valuable work already accomplished by our community, my passion for civic duty means there is still more to be done. As a Harrow Councillor for over 10 years, former advisor to Prime Minister Rishi Sunak and now Chief of Staff to the Conservative Party I have witnessed first-hand the impact it can have to invest in local employers and by making charitable organisations more accessible for voluntary work. Building a strong community starts from the ground up and we must both encourage, uplift and reward older members who chose to re-engage in work.Our report titled: %u2018An Ageing Population in the Lohana community%u2019, demonstrated the real-life implications loneliness can have on our ageing population. It highlighted to many that elderly people are expected to pretend they are %u2018fine%u2019 and that living alone suits them. Which is often caused by the intention of not wanting to become a burden to their family and friends. The report shed light to all but especially children, that loneliness amongst their parents is an increasingly worrying problem. As a result, more children have been encouraging their parents to participate in their business ventures and remain occupied with work.With our ageing population, it is more important than ever before that working is encouraged. Research has shown that staying busy and active over 65 is the key to healthy ageing. Working past the retirement age helps to keep or brains active and live longer.Furthermore, too often in later life we see there is an increasing sense of neglect, portraying a deeper intergenerational problem. What once was a house filled with constant commotion turns into a household still enough to hear a pin drop. This immediate shift can be hard to grasp. This is why it is crucial to exercise our brains every day to tackle the feeling of isolation. Remaining in work supports this, creating a direct knock-on effect that benefits the community and our wider society.Our older community is made up of individuals with endless resilience and the net positive they provide to society is substantial. We must recognise their values of hard work and civic duty even in their age of retirement and use it as motivation for our everyday lives.
